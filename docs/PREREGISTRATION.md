The text from "### 3.3" to the end of §3.6 is cut by `tools/preregister.py` from the build prompt, word for word. ### 3.3 The FAA registry

**Source.** `https://registry.faa.gov/database/ReleasableAircraft.zip`, 60 to 75 MB, refreshed daily at 11:30 p.m. Central. The site sits behind Akamai, which refused a bare `Mozilla/5.0` from a GitHub runner on eight straight weeks to 2026-10-18 and is reported to refuse some home connections too. `src/gdn/faa.py` tries, in order, and keeps the first file whose first two bytes read `PK`:
1. `curl -sSfL --max-time 600 --retry 2` with a full desktop Chrome `User-Agent`, `Accept: text/html,application/xhtml+xml,application/xml;q=0.9,*/*;q=0.8`, `Accept-Language: en-US,en;q=0.9` and `Referer: https://www.faa.gov/licenses_certificates/aircraft_certification/aircraft_registry/releasable_aircraft_download`.
2. On the Mac only: `open -g -a "Google Chrome" <url>`, which downloads through the owner's own browser. Wait up to five minutes for a new `~/Downloads/ReleasableAircraft*.zip`, then move it into place.
3. Otherwise the run has no new snapshot. It says so and carries on with the newest one it has.

A snapshot is named for its download date in America/Chicago, `YYYY-MM-DD`. The raw zip goes to `data/raw/faa/ReleasableAircraft-<date>.zip`, out of git.

**Files and columns.** Read `ACFTREF.txt`, `MASTER.txt` and `DEREG.txt` from the zip, matching names without regard to case. Decode each as UTF-8 with errors replaced. Normalize each header by removing a byte-order mark, upper-casing it and deleting every character that is not a letter or a digit, so `MFR MDL CODE` and `MFR-MDL-CODE` both read `MFRMDLCODE`. The fields: `CODE`, `MFR`, `MODEL` and `TYPEENG` from ACFTREF; `NNUMBER`, `SERIALNUMBER`, `MFRMDLCODE`, `NAME`, `CERTISSUEDATE`, `AIRWORTHDATE` and `YEARMFR` from MASTER; the same plus `CANCELDATE` and `EXPCOUNTRY` from DEREG. `YEARMFR` may be absent; the others may not. The files are fixed-width with a comma after each field, and a name can hold a comma. Split each line on commas with Python's `csv` reader; when that gives a different field count from the header's, cut the line at the header line's comma positions instead. Trim every cell. Skip blank lines, and records whose serial number is blank. Phase 1 records the real header lines in `DECISIONS.md`.

**Dates.** Eight digits as `YYYYMMDD`, which is what the files hold, or the same with one separator, `/` or `-`, used between both pairs of parts. Anything else, or a date that does not exist, is blank.

**Scope.**
1. An ACFTREF code is in scope when its `MFR` or its `MODEL`, upper-cased, contains `GULFSTREAM` and its `TYPEENG` is `4` or `5`. That keeps the G280, which the registry files under the maker `IAI LTD` with the model `GULFSTREAM G280`, and drops Gulfstream's old turboprops and the Grumman-era piston aircraft.
2. Cabin: `mid` when the model contains `G280`, `G150`, `G200` or `G100`, or the maker starts with `IAI` or `ISRAEL`; otherwise `large`.
3. The stored subset of a snapshot is each file's header line plus its in-scope rows, byte for byte and in file order: ACFTREF rows by `CODE`, MASTER and DEREG rows by `MFRMDLCODE`. Write each as `data/snapshots/<date>/<FILE>.txt.gz` with `gzip.compress(data, mtime=0)`. `data/snapshots/MANIFEST.json` reads `{"backtest": "<date>", "snapshots": {"<date>": {"downloaded_utc": ..., "zip_sha256": ..., "zip_bytes": ..., "files": {"ACFTREF.txt.gz": <sha256>, ...}}}}`. The subset stays in git; the raw zip does not.

**Airframes.** An airframe is the first three characters of `MFRMDLCODE` plus the upper-cased serial number. Gather its records from MASTER and DEREG. A record is Gulfstream-held when its `NAME` starts with `GULFSTREAM AEROSPACE`. Then:
- `customer_cert`: the earliest `CERTISSUEDATE` among its records that are not Gulfstream-held.
- `gulfstream_cancel`: the earliest `CANCELDATE` among its Gulfstream-held DEREG records.
- `export_cancel` and `export_country`: the earliest `CANCELDATE` among its Gulfstream-held DEREG records with a non-blank `EXPCOUNTRY`, and that record's country (the alphabetically first when two share the date).
- `held_by_gulfstream`: 1 when a MASTER record is Gulfstream-held, else 0.
- `model` and `cabin` come from the alphabetically first code among its records.
- `first_airworthiness`: the earliest `AIRWORTHDATE` among its records. `year_mfr`: the earliest four-digit `YEARMFR` from 1900 to 2099. `bound`: `first_airworthiness` plus 365 days; with no airworthiness date, December 31 of the year after `year_mfr`; with neither, blank.
- **E1** is the earlier of `customer_cert` and `export_cancel`. **E2** is the earlier of `customer_cert` and `gulfstream_cancel`. Before taking either, drop any input dated after `bound` when `bound` is set. Either is blank when no input remains.

E1 reads a delivery from a customer's own registration or an export. E2 also counts the day Gulfstream cancels its own test registration, which comes sooner for a US buyer but also fires when Gulfstream merely moves a jet to another of its test N-numbers. The bound exists because MASTER keeps only an airframe's current registration: a first owner's certificate renewed after seven years would otherwise read as a new delivery. It also keeps out most used jets, whose airworthiness dates are old. A used jet with no airworthiness date or year, or one whose maker code changed between registrations, can still be miscounted; those go in Limitations. Gulfstream's test registrations use N-numbers ending in `GA` or `GD`; nothing here depends on that.

`data/processed/serials.csv` holds one row per airframe of the backtest snapshot, sorted by maker code then serial, with the columns `mfr_code,serial,model,cabin,held_by_gulfstream,first_airworthiness,year_mfr,bound,customer_cert,gulfstream_cancel,export_cancel,export_country,e1,e2`. Dates are ISO, blank when none. The three date columns `customer_cert`, `gulfstream_cancel` and `export_cancel` are before the bound is applied.

`data/processed/registry_quarterly.csv` has the columns `quarter,window_start,window_end,e1_large,e1_mid,e2_large,e2_mid`, 2016Q1 to 2026Q2. Each count is the airframes of that cabin whose event date falls after `window_start`, through `window_end`.

**Pipeline.** The airframes held by Gulfstream with neither event, counted by model, at the latest snapshot. These are jets in flight test or completion on Gulfstream's own registration.

### 3.4 The models

For quarter q and cabin c, Y is GD's reported count and N the registry count under one definition.
- **seasonal:** Y(q−4). **last:** Y(q−1). **raw:** N(q).
- **ratio:** N(q) × [Y(q−1) + … + Y(q−4)] / [N(q−1) + … + N(q−4)], per cabin. When the four N sum to zero, the trailing mean of Y instead.
- **guidance:** GD's own pace. G is the full-year outlook for q's year from the latest GD earnings call, dated on or before q's fiscal end, that stated a number for it. "About 160" reads 160; a range reads its midpoint; a stated change from a figure named in the same sentence applies to that figure ("five to six fewer than the 145" reads 139.5). A call that confirms the outlook without a number, or gives only a quarter's plan, states none. For quarter k of year t, w_j is quarter j's share of reported deliveries pooled over years t−3 to t−1, and YTD is the reported total of year t's quarters before q. The total is max(0, G − YTD) × w_k / (w_k + … + w_4). Large is that total times the large-cabin share of reported deliveries over q−1 to q−4; mid is the rest. With no numeric outlook for the quarter, guidance falls back to seasonal. It runs in the holdout only.
- `data/guidance.csv` has the columns `call_date,year,outlook,quote,speaker,url`, one row per GD earnings call from 2020-01-29 to 2026-07-29. `year` is the fiscal year the outlook covers, which for every one of these calls is the call's own calendar year. `outlook` is the number read by the rules above, at full precision, blank when the call stated none. The 2025-07-23 call's transcripts read "$150 million to $155 million"; it reads as 150 to 155 aircraft, 152.5. Read every quote on its page; when a page will not load, use another transcript of the same call, and when none loads, say so in `DECISIONS.md` for that row. The gate pins every row through 2026-07-29 and reads later rows as given.
- A model's total is its large plus its mid. Absolute error AE = |total − reported total|; APE = AE / reported total × 100.
- **Design window**, 2017Q1 to 2019Q4 (12 quarters): seasonal, last, and raw and ratio under both E1 and E2. The definition with the lower design MAE of ratio is chosen, E1 on a tie.
- **Holdout**, 2020Q1 to 2026Q2 (26 quarters): seasonal, last, guidance, and raw and ratio under the chosen definition only.
- `results/design.csv` and `results/holdout.csv` have the columns `quarter,window,definition,model,large,mid,total,actual,ae,ape_pct`. `definition` is `none` for seasonal, last and guidance. Floats at full precision.
- **Tests.** For each baseline b in guidance, seasonal and last, over the holdout quarters both score: a one-sided exact sign test that ratio's AE is lower. Ties drop. With w wins among n untied quarters, p = Σ from k = w to n of C(n, k) / 2^n. Apply Holm's step-down procedure at 0.05 across the three. Also report the relative MAE, MAE(ratio) / MAE(b); the lag-1 autocorrelation of D = AE(b) − AE(ratio) in quarter order, computed as Σ from t = 2 to n of (D_t − D̄)(D_{t−1} − D̄) divided by Σ from t = 1 to n of (D_t − D̄)², null under three quarters, since correlated differences weaken the sign test and the README says so when it is positive; and each model's MAE without 2020, as a secondary figure.
- **Headline rule.** The README, the note, the dashboard and the post say ratio beat a baseline only when Holm rejects for it and the relative MAE is below 1. Otherwise they say, with the figures filled in: "Over 26 held-out quarters the registry count was off by X jets a quarter on average, against Y for <baseline>. The difference is not statistically distinguishable." Guidance is the baseline named first when at least 22 holdout quarters have a numeric outlook; otherwise it stays in the table, the README says why, and seasonal takes its place in the headline.
- **Past-miss band.** For each holdout quarter, take the signed errors (ratio total minus reported) of every earlier quarter from 2017Q1 under the chosen definition. P10 and P90 are their nearest-rank percentiles: sorted ascending, the element at position ⌈p × n⌉. The band is [total − P90, total − P10]. Coverage is the count of holdout quarters whose reported total falls inside, ends included, with an exact Clopper–Pearson 95% interval. A live band uses every signed error to date: design, holdout and each scored live quarter's locked error. Call it the past-miss band everywhere, never a confidence interval.
- **Truncation.** For d of 0, 30, 60 and 90 days, ratio's holdout MAE when each quarter's own count keeps only events dated on or before its `window_end` minus d, with its four lags left whole. It stands in, crudely, for a registry that has not finished posting; label it so.
- **Coverage drift.** C(q) is the reported total over the registry total under the chosen definition, for every quarter whose registry total is not zero. Take its mean and sample standard deviation over 2017Q1 to 2025Q2, and flag any of 2025Q3 to 2026Q2 above the mean plus two standard deviations. The README and the note print the flags.
- `results/metrics.json` reads `{"definition", "design": {"n": 12, "mae": {"none": {"seasonal", "last"}, "E1": {"raw", "ratio"}, "E2": {"raw", "ratio"}}}, "holdout": {...}, "band_errors": [...]}`. `holdout` holds `n` (26); `mae`, `mape`, `bias`, `mae_large`, `mae_mid` and `mae_ex_2020`, each `{model: value}` over all five models; `win_rate_vs_seasonal`; `tests`, `{baseline: {"wins", "losses", "ties", "p_one_sided", "holm_reject"}}`; `rel_mae` and `autocorr_lag1`, each `{baseline: value}`; `guidance`, `{"sourced", "fallback_quarters", "mae_sourced", "headline"}`, where `fallback_quarters` lists the quarter labels with no outlook and `headline` is true at 22 or more sourced quarters; `band`, `{"hits", "n", "coverage_pct", "cp95_pct": [low, high], "by_quarter": [{"quarter", "low", "high", "hit"}]}`; `truncation_mae`, `{"0", "30", "60", "90"}`; and `coverage_ratio`, `{"by_quarter": {quarter: value}, "mean", "sd", "flags"}`. `band_errors` lists ratio's signed errors from 2017Q1 to 2026Q2 in quarter order. Bias is the mean of total minus reported; the win rate is the percent of holdout quarters where ratio's AE sits strictly below seasonal's.

### 3.5 The live nowcast

The live model is ratio under the chosen definition. The live quarter is the earliest quarter from 2026Q3 on that is not yet scored and whose four lag quarters all have a reported count. So no 2026Q4 file exists until 2026Q3 is scored.
- A prediction is JSON with the keys `quarter, window_start, window_end, snapshot, definition, events, trailing, nowcast, band, made_utc`. `events` is `{"large", "mid"}` for the quarter; `trailing` is `{"reported": {"large", "mid"}, "registry": {"large", "mid"}}`, each the sum over the four quarters before; `nowcast` is `{"large", "mid", "total"}`; `band` is the past-miss band `[low, high]` of §3.4; `made_utc` reads `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ`. Every count comes from the snapshot the prediction names, the past quarters' included.
- Reported history for a live quarter's lags comes from §3.2, then from each scored live quarter.
- `results/live/<Q>_build.json`: the build session's nowcast from the backtest snapshot, for 2026Q3 only. Written once, never edited.
- `results/live/<Q>_latest.json`: rewritten at each snapshot. `results/live/<Q>_path.csv` gains one row per snapshot date, with the columns `date,events_large,events_mid,trailing_registry_large,trailing_registry_mid,nowcast_large,nowcast_mid,nowcast_total`. Every snapshot a nowcast reads also writes its airframe table, the columns of `serials.csv`, to `data/snapshots/daily/<date>.serials.csv.gz`.
- Path rows keep accruing after the lock, until the quarter is scored. They show how many of the quarter's registrations posted late, which no backtest can show.

### 3.6 The lock and the score

- `config/calendar.json` reads `{"releases": {"2026Q3": "2026-10-30"}, "fiscal_ends": {}, "public": false}`.
- **Lock.** A run locks the live quarter Q on the release date's eve, by the calendar in America/New_York, when its newest snapshot is dated the eve or the time is 19:00 ET or later. It starts with `git pull --no-rebase --tags` and `gh release view lock-<Q>`, and stops at once when that release exists. From seven days before each release date, every snapshot stores its full subset under `data/snapshots/<date>/` with a manifest entry. The run first commits and pushes its own daily outputs, subsets included. It then recomputes the prediction from the newest snapshot that has a stored subset, writes it to `predictions/<Q>.json`, commits that file alone and pushes. When the push is rejected, it runs `git fetch` and `git reset --hard @{u}`, which drops only the unpushed lock commit and rewrites nothing on GitHub, and starts again from `gh release view`, three attempts at most. Then it runs `gh release create lock-<Q> --target <that commit> --title "Locked nowcast <Q>" --notes "sha256 <hex of the file's bytes>"` and `git fetch origin refs/tags/lock-<Q>:refs/tags/lock-<Q>`. It writes `results/live/<Q>_lock.json`, `{"tag", "published_at", "sha256", "commit"}`, from `gh release view lock-<Q> --json publishedAt,tagName` and `git rev-list -n 1 lock-<Q>`, commits and pushes that, and prints the sha256 in backticks with one line asking the owner to post it somewhere public. When a later run finds the release but no `_lock.json`, it fetches the tag and writes the file instead of stopping. The prediction file is committed once and never changed. The release's `published_at` must fall before 00:00 ET on the release date, and the snapshot date before the release date. GitHub sets a release's `created_at` from its target commit, so only `published_at` proves the time. One lock per quarter. Every lock stays in the Live record for good, hit or miss. A quarter that misses the window goes without a lock: say so, and never lock late. Call a lock a timestamped GitHub release until `config/calendar.json` carries `"public": true`, which the owner sets once the repository is public or the sha256 is posted publicly.
- **Score.** When the quarter's results 8-K appears (§3.2's rule), parse its counts and write `results/live/<Q>_score.json`: `{"quarter", "reported": {"large", "mid", "total"}, "accn", "locked", "nowcast", "ae_total", "ape_pct", "inside_band", "guidance_pace", "guidance_ae"}`. `nowcast` is the lock's `{"large", "mid", "total"}` object; without a lock, `locked` is false and `nowcast`, `ae_total`, `ape_pct` and `inside_band` are null. `guidance_pace` is the guidance model's total for the quarter by §3.4 and `guidance_ae` its AE, or null without an outlook.

## Chosen definition

The design window, 2017Q1 to 2019Q4 (12 quarters), selects **E1**. The ratio model's design MAE is 5.472548294344893 aircraft under E1 and 5.5807858422376135 under E2; the lower wins, E1 on a tie. The holdout scores ratio and raw under E1 only.
