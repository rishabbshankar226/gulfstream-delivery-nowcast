  The preregistration says "the latest GD earnings call, dated on or before q's fiscal end, that stated a number for it". The README text in my brief says "the full-year outlook from GD's latest call".
  Reading A (my primary): the latest call among those that stated a number. A call that only confirms the outlook is skipped and the earlier number stands.
    sourced quarters 22; fallback quarters 2021Q1, 2021Q2, 2021Q3, 2021Q4 (they fall back to same quarter last year).
    holdout MAE of guidance 6.917259; MAE over its 22 sourced quarters 7.356760 (ratio 5.250326, seasonal 4.636364 over the same 22).
  Reading B: the latest call on or before the fiscal end must itself state the number; when it does not, the quarter has no outlook.
    sourced quarters 16; fallback adds 2022Q2, 2022Q4, 2024Q2, 2025Q2, 2025Q4, 2026Q2.
    holdout MAE of guidance 6.683550; MAE over its 16 sourced quarters 8.423269 (ratio 5.669689, seasonal 5.062500 over the same 16).
  Why it matters: the headline rule names guidance first only at 22 or more sourced quarters. Reading A gives exactly 22, the minimum. Reading B gives 16, and seasonal would lead the headline. Under either reading Holm rejects nothing.
  For 2026Q3 both readings give the same pace: outlook 160 from the 2026-07-29 call, 33.840000.

## Observations outside the list (facts my commands printed; the main session decides what they mean)

O1. The baseline that wins. On holdout MAE, same quarter last year is the best of the five: 4.615385, against 5.523395 for ratio (relative MAE 1.196736; ratio wins 11 of 26, loses 15). Ratio is ahead of guidance pace (6.917259) and last quarter (8.807692) on MAE, but Holm rejects for none, so by the headline rule nothing may be called a beat. Ratio's bias is +2.235614 jets; its MAE by year runs from 3.037920 (2024) to 7.025277 (2021).

O2. Used jets inside the count. By the registry's own YEAR MFR, 246 of the 1251 E1 events in 2016Q1 to 2026Q2 belong to airframes three or more years old at the event (large 214, mid 32); 76 more have no year. 929 are zero to two years old. By model the 246 include GV-SP (G550) 69, GVI (G650ER) 32, GIV-X (G450) 30, GVI (G650) 20, G-IV 17, G-V 12. Section 21 has the table by year. Some of the 246 may be true first deliveries of older test or demonstrator airframes (GVII-G500 10, GVII-G600 5, GVIII-G700 3); I cannot tell those apart from the registry.
  In the locked quarter, 2026Q3, 5 of the 42 events are three or more years old by YEAR MFR: G550 serial 5260 (2009), G-1159A serial 445 (1984), G650ER serial 6206 (2016), G150 serial 311 (2014), and G700 serial 87118 (year reads 2006, which I take to be a data-entry error: its serial sits among the 2026 G700s, 87114 to 87128). A sixth, G-IV serial 1335, has no year and no airworthiness date, so no bound applies. For 5260, 445, 6206 and 311 the registry's AIR WORTH DATE is in July to September 2026, within weeks of the new certificate, so the bound does not exclude them. That is consistent with a used jet getting a new airworthiness date when it enters or re-enters the US registry; I did not check the FAA's definition of the field.
  In fairness to the README sentence "which keeps out renewed certificates and most used jets": the bound drops the customer certificate of 1879 airframes (section 13), so "most" holds as worded. What the sentence leaves out is that 246 of the 1251 counted events (19.664269 percent), plus 76 of unknown year (6.075140 percent), are still airframes three or more years old by the registry's own year of manufacture.
  Defensible version: "The bound keeps out renewed certificates and most used jets, but not all: about one counted event in five is an airframe at least three years old, most often a used jet that got a new airworthiness date."

O3. A later registry copy also loses old events. Between the 2026-10-19 and 2026-10-29 snapshots, three past E1 events vanished: G450 serial 4166 (2019Q3), G650ER serial 6379 (2019Q4) and G280 serial 2053 (2024Q4). In the later snapshot each carries a customer certificate dated 2026-10-20 to 2026-10-27, past its bound, and the earlier event date no longer comes out of the records. One event appeared: G150 serial 311 in 2026Q3; in the earlier snapshot its 2026-08-26 certificate fell outside the bound, in the later one the airframe carries an airworthiness date of 2026-09-02 and the event counts. Six past-quarter cells changed in ten days (section 14).
  In the backtest snapshot, registry events per reported delivery rise almost every year: 0.520661 (2016), 0.650000, 0.661157, 0.795918, 0.740157, 0.957983, 1.133333, 1.054054, 1.088235, 1.215190 (2025), 1.417722 (2026, two quarters). From 2022 on the registry shows more "first" events than GD reported deliveries.
  So the framing "a registry copy that had months or years to fill in" is half the picture: the copy also had years to lose first registrations as jets changed hands. The counts for old quarters are lower than a snapshot taken at the time would have shown; I cannot say by how much from two snapshots ten days apart.
